# LifeLoop – Game Design (MVP)

This document describes the game **as implemented** in `src/engine` and `src/data`, with the tuning numbers that are actually in the code, plus the post-MVP roadmap. It was informed by research into BitLife, Verdant, Another Life and Mind Vacation's Life Simulator (see §20) and a design panel; where the panel's proposals differ from what shipped, the roadmap says why.

## 1. Vision and pillars

A text life simulator where **one tap is one year**, every year asks a question, and every life ends with a story worth sharing. Three pillars:

1. **Consequence over grind.** Choices move stats, money, relationships and karma; there are no timers, no energy that regenerates in real time, no content paywall.
2. **A life you can read back.** The journal, the ribbon, the obituary and the graveyard turn a run into a story.
3. **Fair monetization.** Ads are rare and never interrupt a decision; one premium purchase removes them and adds perks; "what is free forever" is published in the listing and in Settings.

Working title "LifeLoop"; Teen / 13+ content rating target.

## 2. Core loop

**Between age-ups** the player can act freely (study, work actions, interact with people, buy things, activities, crimes) limited by per-year caps and a small **energy** budget (1 at ages 0-5, 2 at 6-12, 4 from 13, 3 from 60) that gates social/physical actions so a year is a small budgeting puzzle (Another Life's idea, trimmed). Money and admin actions are free and Age+ is never blocked.

**Age up** (`src/engine/ageUp.ts`) runs these phases in this order:

1. Advance age and year; reset per-year counters and energy.
2. Milestone line (13, 18, 21, 30, 40, 50, 65, 80, 90, 100; a birthday behind bars has its own line, "Half a century old, and I spent the birthday behind bars.", and on the birthday a sentence ends the usual line comes after the release in step 4), then the starting scenario's rules (§4a: an immigrant family's move abroad, an orphan aging out of foster care).
3. Stat drift (§3).
4. Prison year (if imprisoned): release, or a parole hearing card once half is served; outside, parole counts down, and at 18 a juvenile record is sealed (§14).
5. Education (§6).
6. Career and side hustle (§7, §8).
7. Assets: value drift, wear, mortgage amortisation (§11).
8. Finance: income, taxes, expenses, debt, bankruptcy (§9).
9. Health: recoveries, new illness roll, addiction drift (§13).
10. NPCs age; relationship decay; NPC deaths and inheritance (§10).
11. Relationship events: break-ups, new friends, new partners (§10); then the player's children's milestones and any grandchildren born (§10a).
12. Job listings refresh (from age 14).
13. Mortality roll and the critical-health countdown (§5).
14. One flavour line (§15a), chosen once step 15 has scheduled the year's cards: always in a quiet year (so no year is empty), sometimes a situational one in a busy year.
15. Schedule 0-3 random events (§15) and open the first one as a card.
16. The app shows a one-line **year summary** (stat deltas, cash change) once the year's cards are closed; its headline is picked from the whole year, the cards' outcomes included (never the card text itself), and a card's outcome, as the year's latest news, wins a tie with the year's own lines, so the recap does not contradict the cards. A line that was also last year's is skipped, except a milestone (a second parole in a row is still this year's release). Two kinds of news outrank everything: the death of a parent, step-parent, spouse, partner or child (never "I started middle school." in the year Mom died), then a card that sent the player to prison, or back to prison after a release that year (never "I walked out of prison" under "In prison · 2 years left"); `src/store/yearRecap.ts`.

A card must be answered before anything else happens. Resolving it may queue follow-ups. Each queued card's age range and conditions are checked again when it opens, and a card that no longer applies (the player was jailed, lost the job, got a pet) is skipped.

## 4. Character generation

Random gender, name from the birth country's name pool (9 pools; a few names are in both the first-name and the surname lists, so nobody's first name is drawn as their own surname, and nobody in the family is named after the family's surname: no Bello Bello, no Grandpa Laurent in the Laurent family), city, birth year 1975-2010 (the era is part of the story), zodiac, 0-2 **traits** from 10 (quick learner, charming, iron constitution, lucky, hot-headed, thrifty, creative, athletic, anxious, rebellious), mother (18-42) and usually a father (88%), 0-3 older siblings (35/40/18/7%; each parent at home was at least 17 when the eldest was born, so a parent of 18 has at most one), 0-4 living grandparents (§10a; usually 1-3), family wealth: wealthy 8%, poor 20%. The parents' jobs fit the family's money: a poor family has no accountant, software developer, pharmacist, lawyer, dentist, engineer or architect at home, and a wealthy family's parents (a mother of 25-42, a father of 23 or more) hold one of those professions or run a shop or sell real estate. Thirteen birth countries each set currency, salary multiplier (0.1-1.0), cost of living, school ages, drinking age, public healthcare, gambling legality, life expectancy and tax rate.

Custom life (free): name, gender, country and a starting scenario (§4a). Premium: starting stats and a wealthy family (the Wealthy scenario).

## 4a. Starting scenarios

The new-life screen offers eight starts as cards (`src/data/scenarios.ts`); Standard is the default and Wealthy is the only premium one (locked cards open the paywall; the store also refuses a Wealthy start without premium). The scenario is stored on the character (`character.scenario`, absent for heirs and older saves) and as the flag `scenario:<id>`, so events can condition on `{ flag: 'scenario:orphan' }`. A Standard birth draws exactly the same life as before scenarios existed. Rules live in `createLife` (`systems/character.ts`) and `systems/scenarios.ts`.

| Scenario | At birth | Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | today's random birth (§4) | — |
| Orphan | no parents or grandparents; a foster parent aged 30-55 of modest means (kind stepmother/stepfather marked `foster`, shown as "Foster mother"/"Foster father"; they pay a minor's costs and take them to the doctor like a parent), 0-2 foster siblings (40/40/20%) with their own surnames; flag `foster_care`; never wealthy | at 18: "I aged out of foster care." (one still in juvenile detention on their birthday ages out on release day that year); a foster parent who dies is replaced by a new one ("With Kian gone, my caseworker placed me with a new foster mother, Sara Lee.") |
| Big family | 3-5 older siblings with distinct names, mother aged 26-42; always `poorFamily` | — |
| Single parent | one parent: a mother 75%, a father 25% ("She's raising me on her own."); grandparents only on that side | — |
| Immigrant | a planned move (`character.moveAbroad`): age 6-14, to another country, three times as likely to be one with a higher salary multiplier | at that age (not in detention, a parent or step-parent alive: an aunt or uncle who took the child in does not carry out the move): country and city change, classmates and all but the two closest friends (a best friend first) stay behind and leave the life, so the new country has room for new friends; those two, rivals and grandparents stay behind as people who live far away (`farAway`: a person selector binds them only with `farAway: true`, which binds only them, for a letter or a call, or `farAway: 'any'`, for a message or group chat that works at any distance; `hasPerson`/`noPerson` count only people nearby; so no sleepover or weekend at Grandma's binds them), −4 happiness, a milestone line ("My family moved from Mexico to Canada. | Prodigy | Smarts 85-100 (even over lower custom stats), the Quick learner trait (still at most two rolled traits), flag `prodigy` | — |
| Farm kid | a farm town of the birth country (four or five per country; "a small town near <city>" for a country without a list), the father (or a single mother) is a farmer and the mother often too, flag `farm` | — |

Parent-bound events open for whoever is raising the child: the early-childhood events whose text is parent-neutral also bind a step-parent or foster parent, so an orphan's first years are not empty (measured over 80 lives per scenario: ages 0-5 average 0.64 events a year for an orphan vs 0.65 for a Standard life). Content written for a scenario should condition on its flag (`foster_care`, `immigrant`, `farm`, `prodigy` or `scenario:<id>`); the validator and the text audit sample two to three lives of each scenario.

## 5. Aging and mortality

Yearly death hazard = base(age) × (78 / country life expectancy)² × health factor + illness/addiction add-ons:

- base: <1: 0.4% · 1-14: 0.03% · 15-24: 0.08% · 25-44: 0.15% · 45-54: 0.4% · 55-64: 0.9% · 65-74: 2% · 75-84: 5% · 85-94: 12% · 95-104: 25% · 105+: 50%
- health factor: ≤10 ×6, <25 ×3.5, <40 ×2, ≥80 ×0.7
- severity-5 illness +12% (treated +4%), severity-4 +4% (treated +1%); addiction ≥60 +1%; prison +0.4%

**Critical countdown:** when health ≤ 8 the doctor warns the player; two years later without recovery the life ends ("complications from X"). Recovering above 8 clears it with a "second chance" line. The birthday of the warning skips the ordinary death roll, so the player always gets a year to act on it.

Simulated outcomes (`npm test` enforces them): passive play median death ≈ 62, balanced play ≈ 78 (must be ≥ 70), reckless ≈ 55; nobody passes 120.

## 6. Education

Country school start age → primary → middle (11) → high (14) → graduation at the country's high-school end age (17-19). Grade starts at 50 + (Smarts−50)/2 and moves each year by (Smarts−50)/10 ± 5, +10 for Study harder, −8 per Skip class (25% chance of being caught: a minor's parents nearby hear about it, while a grown university or graduate student's professor and grade take the hit; 1 energy, twice a year), −3 rebellious, −4 when very unhappy. Graduation needs grade ≥ 30 (honours ≥ 85). Dropping out ends schooling and is only possible from high school or later and from age 16. University and graduate school need a high school diploma and cannot be started from prison.

University: 25 majors with Smarts thresholds (25-65), 4-5 years, tuition $11-16k/yr (scaled) added to student debt unless on a **scholarship** (60% if grade ≥ 85 and Smarts ≥ 70; 40% if Smarts ≥ 90) or born wealthy (the family pays: `education.parentsPay`, which is not a scholarship; only while a parent or step-parent is alive and for a student under 30). Graduate: medical school, law school, dental school, MBA, master's, PhD with major prerequisites. Student debt is repaid at 10%/yr (min $2k) once out of school.

## 7. Careers

71 job ladders (3-6 rungs) in 13 categories; 6 part-time teen jobs. Each listing rolls a company name and ±8-12% salary. Requirements: education level or specific professional degree, accepted majors, Smarts/Looks/Health minimums, years of experience, no criminal record. Interview success = 65% + (Smarts−50)/200 + (Looks−50)/400, +10% charming, −10% with a record (clamped 15-95%).

Yearly: performance drifts ±7 ± (Smarts−50)/20, −5 when unhappy or unhealthy, −8 with an addiction; **Work harder** +8 once a year; raise 1-5% when performance ≥ 65, capped at 125% of the rung's base pay (only a promotion moves the cap); a part-time job kept past 24 moves to full hours: its pay rises to at least an entry-level full-time wage of $24k and raises can take it to $30k; promotion chance 30% when performance ≥ 72 and 2+ years at the rung; **Ask for a raise** (60%/35%/10% by performance) and **Ask for promotion** (55%/40%/8%) once a year; fired at 50% when performance < 18; 2% random layoff (recorded as laid off, not a firing: it does not count toward `firings`; never for an elected or appointed office, a judge or the armed forces: `JobDef.noLayoffsFrom`); injury chance = danger/400; stress reduces Happiness/Health each year; fame jobs add fame. Retirement from 60 pays 35% of the salary retired from (the best one, if retired more than once); a retiree who takes a new job is paid that job instead, and the pension resumes when it ends. Every retirement is a work-history entry (reason `retired`, ending at the age retired), so `{ sinceRetired }` counts from the latest one, is false while a retiree works again or after that job ends any other way, and starts over when they retire again; retiring happens during a year, so the first card that can follow it opens at the next birthday with `sinceRetired` = 1. Prison ends employment (the work history says "went to prison"). A playing career has an age window (`JobDef.careerEnds`, `JobRequirements.maxAge`): athletes are signed up to 36, retire from sport between 34 and 40 (sooner if their health gives out) with a line about the last game, and go on to other work without a pension; the ladder tops out at Star Athlete, and a star of four or more years is voted into the Hall of Fame five years after the last game (flag `hallOfFame`, named in the obituary). Listings hold only jobs of their year (`JobDef.minYear`: web designers from 1995, data scientists from 2008), and a few rungs are named for the country and the era (`rungTitle` in `src/data/jobs.ts`): the politician's national offices are Congress and the Senate in the United States, the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate in Mexico and Brazil, the House of Representatives and the Senate in Nigeria, the Diet in Japan, the Bundestag in Germany and Parliament (then a minister's post) elsewhere, and a TV chef starts as a food columnist before 2004 rather than a food blogger.

## 8. Side hustles and investing

Six hustles in any year (streaming, indie apps, craft shop, rideshare – needs a car and ends once no car is owned, tutoring, furniture flipping), levels 0-5. The internet ones wait for their era (`minYear`; `util.inEra`): a streaming channel from 2007, an app studio from 2008, the online craft shop from 1999 and rideshare from 2012; before them the menu offers wedding videos, computer repair, a craft stall at weekend markets and pizza delivery (needs a car), which retire (`maxYear`) once the newer one arrives. One already running carries on in any year. A hustle takes its energy (1) when started and again from each new year's budget while it runs; none can be started from prison, where a running one is paused and earns nothing. Level-up chance = growth × (0.3 + stat/120) (+15% creative trait on creative hustles); 8% chance to slip a level; income by level ($0 → $15k-180k) ±20%; viral bonus chance 2-6% from level 2 (an offline hustle has its own breakout line, and nothing "goes viral" before 2005); level 5 sets the Hustler ribbon flag. The journal says when a hustle slips ("had a slow year and slipped back to level 2"), announces a level only the first time it is reached ("It reached level 3", kept as `Hustle.peak`) and calls one won back after a slip a recovery ("bounced back to level 3").

Investments (from 18): savings (2%), index fund (normal 7% ± 15%, clamped −45..+60%), crypto (normal 12% ± 60%, clamped −80..+250%; on offer from 2011, and one already held can always be withdrawn). Invest 25% of cash per tap; withdraw all. Returns accrue to the balance (unrealised until withdrawn) and never move cash on their own, except that a year whose bills cash cannot cover draws on them first (§9).

## 9. Finances and expenses

Income = salary (×2 in a windfall-boost year; 0 in prison; pension when retired) + hustle income − taxes + passive (rent at 3.5-6% of a rented-out home's value; investment returns stay in the investment balance). Tax = gross × country rate × min(1, 0.6 + gross/250k).

Adult expenses: housing $7k (own home) / $2.5k (living with parents, under 24 and earning < $20k) / $9k (renting), plus lifestyle spending of 30% of gross income above $12k (capped at $50k) so a salary alone does not compound into a fortune; −15% thrifty trait (housing, lifestyle and wealth spending); $4k per child under 18; asset upkeep; mortgage payments; student loan repayments; debt: 15% interest, repaid at 20%/yr (min $1k). Debt over $60k that went unpaid this year with no income at all (rent counts as income) costs 4 Happiness a year; the journal says so when it starts (or starts again) and then only every 5 years while it lasts, in rotating words (`DEBT_CALLS`), and never from a prison cell (a stretch that goes on after release starts over). A second bankruptcy says "again", a third says so, and later ones take turns in new words (`bankruptcyLine`, counted by the number flag `bankruptcies`).

Spending responds to the player's situation (constants in `SPENDING`, `src/engine/systems/finance.ts`):

- **Frugality.** While you carry any debt (student loans aside) you spend nothing on lifestyle extras, and until your savings (cash + investments) cover a year of expenses (housing + children + full lifestyle), extras shrink in proportion (half a year saved → half the extras). A worker climbing out of a jobless stretch therefore pays debt down instead of spiralling into it.
- **Wealth effect.** Once your savings (cash + investments − debt) pass $250k, you spend 10% of the excess each year on a richer life, but never more than that year's spare income: savings level off near $250k + 10 × your yearly surplus instead of growing for 40 years, and the wealth effect never eats into savings or creates debt. Houses and other assets are not counted, and investment returns still compound, so investing remains the way to build a fortune.

Measured with `scripts/balance-report.ts --n 300` (all countries / US), balanced play: solvent at 35 96%, millionaire at 60 15.5% / 19.8%, bankrupt 1.3% / 2.0%, deficit work-years 6.3% / 5.4%, Rich ribbon 9.7% / 11.7%; grinder millionaire at 60 18.0% / 22.4%, bankrupt 1.0% / 0.3%. Cash never goes negative: a year's shortfall is paid out of investments first (savings account, then index fund, then crypto; the journal says so when such a stretch starts, if the year's draw is $1,000 or more), and only the rest becomes **debt** (as event costs and fines do). Minors never pay for events: an event cost for a player under 18 comes out of a living parent's or step-parent's money (richest first) and anything they cannot cover is waived, and any debt a minor does carry is charged no interest or repayments and costs no happiness until 18. A grown-up who moved back in with their parents on a card (flag `home_with_parents`, conditions.ts `movedBackHome`) pays the living-with-parents housing while a parent or step-parent nearby is alive, until a home, a wedding, moving in with a partner, a move to another city or prison ends it. Debt over $250k must be settled. A player who owns enough sells or cashes in first (`settleDebts`): cash and investments pay it down as far as they go, then as few other assets as it takes are sold (the smallest sale that covers what is left over $250k, else the biggest, and again) until the debt is back under $250k; the home is sold only when everything else together would not do, and then first ("With my debts piling up, I cashed in $950,000 from my index fund to pay them off."). Only a player whose everything cannot bring the debt under $250k goes bankrupt: everything but the home is lost (cash and investments included), debt cleared, −15 Happiness. All dollar figures are scaled by the country's salary multiplier. Germany, France, Spain and Italy show the currency the euro replaced (marks, francs, pesetas, lire, at the euro's fixed rate: `CountryDef.preEuro`) for every year before the euro notes and coins of 2002 (`EURO_SINCE`; `util.money` for the journal, card text's `{{$N}}` and every screen, which pass the life's year to `formatMoney`/`formatMoneyFull`; the graveyard and the tombstone use the year of death); game amounts never change. Content writes an amount after its noun ("a fine of {{$300}}", never "a {{$300}} fine", which would read "a 1,400 francs fine" before the euro).

## 10. Relationships

People: parents, step-parents, grandparents, siblings, children, grandchildren, friends/best friends, lovers, spouses, exes, coworkers, classmates, enemies, pets. Each has relationship 0-100 (lovers/spouses also **love** 0-100), looks/smarts/health, craziness (volatility), occupation, money, and a **memory** (last interaction age, insults, compliments, gifts, fights, years neglected).

Yearly: neglected people (no interaction during the year just played) lose relationship (−2 family, −4 friends/partners) and partners lose 5 love; partners with love < 20 leave (40%/yr); friends under 15 drift away; new friends appear (40%/yr under 25, 20% after, max 4 nearby: friends left behind far away do not count), aged by `friendAgeRange` (`systems/character.ts`: a child under 13 befriends children within a year of their own age and at least 3, a teen someone within two years, an adult someone within three and at least 16; a friend an event brings a child follows the same band unless the card asks for a grown-up); a new partner appears for singles 16-74 (25% + looks/400), of the other sex nine times in ten for a player with no history, then leaning to who the player stays with: every year with a partner met before that year counts for that partner's sex (`Character.partnerYears`), and a partner of the player's own sex comes with chance (0.1 + the years with one of their own sex) / (1 + all partner years), kept between 3% and 95% (`sameSexChance`; a year with one makes it about even, five about 85%; a partner left within the year they met counts for nothing; a partner a card brings without a stated sex follows the same leaning), aged by the legal band in `systems/romance.ts` (no romance under 16; a 16-17-year-old meets someone exactly their own age, so the couple turns 18 together; adults meet adults, age −4 to +4); NPCs age and can die (age/health curve); parents/spouses leave inheritances (80% split among children, or 100% to a spouse; a child under 18 always inherits, into their own savings: "It went into my savings."). An event that kills someone (`personDies`) runs the same death handling as a natural death: the journal line, grief, inheritance and, for a child who loses their last parent, being taken in by relatives. NPC causes fit the age: under 18 an accident or an illness (never a heart attack, a stroke or kidney failure), under 35 the young-adult causes, and "natural causes" only from 60. **Grief window:** in the year a lover or spouse dies nobody new becomes a partner, neither by the yearly roll nor through an event card (a date the player takes on a card that year comes to nothing, and the journal says it was too soon), and the yearly roll is half as likely the year after (`systems/romance.ts`); for those two years the flag `grieving` holds the player's age at the loss, so content can wait it out with `{ flag: 'grieving', exists: false }`. Every death records the player's age at it (`Person.lostAtAge`), so a card about the first months of a loss binds the person lost with `{ alive: false, diedWithin: 1 }` (the casseroles card grieves the spouse who just died, never one who died years before a later partner). **Contact:** any interaction that goes ahead (a break-up or a refused proposal included), any event card or journal flavor line bound to someone, a partner telling the player they are leaving, and the goodbye at a move abroad (to the friends and grandparents left behind) counts as time with them that year: it ends a silence on their page ("You haven't spoken in 3 years.") and spares them that year's neglect decay. The page never shows a silence for someone the player sees every day (`seesDaily`, `systems/relationships.ts`; never from prison): anyone they live with by the engine's own rule (`livesWithPlayer`: the parents and siblings of the home they still live in by `livesAtHome`, a spouse, a partner moved in with (`lf_living_together`), pets and their children who have not moved out, so a card's `household` binds a grown child still at home), and a coworker at their current job. The yearly pass records the last such year (`memory.togetherUntil`, as does a coworker's last day at the job), so once they part, after a move, a divorce or a job change, a silence counts from then (an older save without the record counts a childhood home until 18); nor does a page show a silence older than the newest journal line about them, which it lists below. **Growing up:** anyone who joined the life as a student (a sibling, a partner met at 17, a friend) and is still one at 23 gets an adult job (or none) and savings of their own at the next birthday; the player's own children and grandchildren keep to their milestones (§10a). A person's page lists memories only from the year they met the player on. **Surnames:** someone new who is not family (a friend, a partner, a coworker, anyone an event introduces) never gets a surname of the player's family (`familySurnames`), so they do not read as a relative. A partner who takes the player's surname at the wedding (an even chance) keeps the one they were born with as `birthLastName`, which an heir's family tree carries over, so their side of the family keeps its name. **Names:** every new person (a sibling or parent at birth, a friend, partner, baby, coworker, pet, or anyone an event introduces) gets a first name nobody in the life has, alive or remembered (the player, family and the dead, partners and exes, friends, coworkers, pets, the children's spouses), nor the player's surname (so a baby or grandchild who carries it, or a partner who takes it at the wedding, is never Thomas Thomas), while the country's pool still has one (`namesInLife` and `freshFirstName` in `systems/character.ts`; one draw either way, so everything rolled after a name uses the same dice). A name whose owner leaves the life during an age-up (a coworker gone with the job, a friend who drifted apart) stays taken for the rest of that age-up (its new friends, partners, grandchildren and children's spouses), as does one whose owner leaves in a card's choice (a friend who moves away and drifts off) for the rest of that choice, and a name this year's journal mentions, or one in the life at this year's birthday (`namesThisYear`, so also someone who left without a word), is not given to a coworker, a baby, a child's spouse or anyone a card introduces before the next birthday (`namesInJournalThisYear`: the old job's Lucy, left behind on a job change, is not hired again that year); a child's spouse or baby never shares a name with the player or that household (the child, their spouse, their children) even once the pool has run out; a grandchild named after the player is the one deliberate exception.

**Orphan care** (`takeInOrphan`, `systems/aging.ts`). A player under 18 left with no living parent, step-parent or guardian is taken in once, after every death of the year has been announced (so two parents dying the same year write one line): a foster child by a new foster parent (`foster`), anyone else by an aunt or uncle, the brother or sister of a parent they lost (kind stepmother/stepfather marked `relative`, linked by `viaId`, with that side's surname and, for an immigrant family, a first name from the old country; 60% an aunt; within eight years of the parent who just died, 18+ years older than the child and 18+ years younger than that side's grandparents). Like any parent they pay a minor's costs, take them to the doctor and are who `{{parents}}` names ("Aunt Rosa"); people screens and the obituary call them "Aunt"/"Uncle" (`fosterRelation`). The line names who was lost: "With Mom gone, I went to live with her sister, Aunt Rosa." for a child who only ever had one parent (or "With Joe gone, ..." for a step-parent), "With both my parents gone, I went to live with Dad's brother, Uncle Joe." only for a child who had two; in detention the relative "became my guardian". An heir whose only parent was the last player is taken in on the first day of the new life, by the last player's own brother or sister when one is alive and grown (18-75 and ten or more years older than the heir, not a foster sibling; the closest to the last player, carried over as they were and known since birth), otherwise by an aunt or uncle found as above; an older save's orphan is taken in at the next birthday. Neither line tells the loss again ("I went to live with Mom's brother, Uncle Walt."; "My caseworker placed me with a new foster mother, Sara Lee."). The person screen says whose sibling they are and when ("Mom's sister. Took you in when you were 9.", or "Took you in when Mom died." for one known since birth). If the aunt or uncle dies too, another relative steps in.

15 interactions with energy/money costs and yearly caps: spend time, visit (grandparents only; 1 energy, once a year; a player under 18 often leaves with $10-60), conversation (argument chance from craziness and low relationship), compliment, insult (retaliation chance), gift ($150), ask for money (a grandparent from age 10, $20-300), give money ($1k; for a grandchild under 16 it goes into savings), date ($120), propose ($2.5k; needs love ≥ 55), break up, divorce (30% of cash settlement), try for a baby (55%/yr while fertile; same-sex couples adopt), play with a pet (in the way its species plays: a ball for a dog, a string for a cat, seeds by the window for a pigeon) and walk a dog (dogs only). Dates need a legal pair (adults with adults, minors only with minors within two years); proposals and babies need both to be 18+. Nobody has a conversation with, compliments or insults a baby or toddler (under 5), whose time and gifts read like it ("I made faces at Grace until she giggled."), or asks anyone under 16 for money; money given to a child, grandchild, brother or sister under 16 goes into savings. Someone who lives far away (§4a; also a friend who moved away or was met far from home, and the parents and siblings a grown-up leaves behind by moving to another city with a card's `relocate`, with children who moved out, their children (and any born to them later), a partner not lived with and an ex, while a child's household moves with them (a partner married from far away moves in); close coworkers stay on as friends far away, the home lived in is sold (or rented out when it is worth less than its mortgage), and a farm kid is no longer on the farm) is reached by phone, mail or a transfer: spending time, visits, dates and trying for a baby are not offered ("They live far away now."; a far-away partner who accepts a proposal moves in at the wedding), the other lines say how ("I called Grandma Rosa, who had saved up a whole year of stories for me."; "I mailed Diego a gift."; a grandparent's money is sent, never pressed into a hand), their page says "Lives far away.", and an event's relationship change for everyone of a kind leaves them out. Options that are unavailable are hidden on the person screen. Event babies are linked to the player's partner as their other parent; step-parents are aged from the parent they marry and are always 16+ years older than the player.

## 10a. Extended family and coworkers

Constants live in `src/engine/systems/family.ts` and `coworkers.ts`; grandparents are rolled in `createLife` (`systems/character.ts`).

- **Grandparents.** At birth each parent has up to two living parents of their own (a grandmother and a grandfather), 20-35 years older than that parent, alive with a chance that falls with age (≤55: 75% · ≤65: 60% · ≤75: 40% · ≤85: 20% · older: 7%), so a newborn has about 2.2 on average (1-3 for 80% of lives, fewer when the parents are older). They carry that parent's surname (the mother's maiden name on her side), health for their age, and are linked to the parent (`viaId`); siblings share them. The birth journal names them ("Grandma Rosa and Grandpa Joe were first in line to hold me."). They age and die with the NPC mortality model: the family death line ("Grandma Rosa died of a stroke at 81."; a parent is "Mom died..."), grief −8 (half when not close; a parent is −15), 60% of their money to the parent on that side and a 15% bequest split between the player and siblings when close (or 30% of the time anyway); a minor's bequest goes into their own savings. Narration calls them "Grandma Rosa" / "Grandpa Joe" (`personRef`). An heir's grandparents are the dead player's living parents, as they were (job, looks, smarts, health).
- **Your children growing up** (engine milestones, one first-person line each): school at the country's school age ("My daughter Maya started school."), the end of high school at the country's end age (80% graduate, +3 happiness; the rest drop out quietly, and an heir who dropped out has no diploma), moving out at 19-26 (an even chance each year, certain by 26; "The house is very quiet." when the last one leaves), marrying at 24-40 (3%/yr, about 40%; the spouse is a name, not a person, of the other gender nine times in ten and always of their own once the child has come out: `childMilestone: 'cameOut'`, or the journal entry of `lf_child_comes_out` in an older save), and children of their own from 22 to 42 (8%/yr married, 4% single, at most 3 per child). Saves from before milestones count a child past 26 as moved out.
- **Grandchildren.** Born to the player's children (age 0, the child's surname, linked by `viaId`) with a milestone line ("My son Diego and his partner had a daughter, Lucia. I am a grandparent."; two husbands or two wives adopt) and +10 happiness for the first, +5 after; they set the `grandparent` flag and count up the `grandchildren` flag. They age, can be spent time with, given gifts and money, appear under Family, and are named in the obituary. An heir who had children keeps them (and their spouse) in the new life. An heir of two fathers or two mothers tells them apart: the late player is "Dad"/"Mom" and the other parent "Papa"/"Mama" (`personRef` with the family; `{{parents}}` reads "Dad and Papa").
- **Events agree with the milestones.** A child-bound event can make a milestone happen itself (`childMilestone: 'movedOut' | 'married' | 'expecting'`, optionally `namedAfterMe`); a baby on the way is born the next year. Child selectors filter on `movedOut`, `married` and `kids` (born plus on the way), so a move-out or wedding card only reaches a child still at home or single, and an announcement never passes three.
- **Coworkers.** A full-time hire brings 2-3 coworkers (titles from the same career ladder up to a rung above the player's, each of an age for their rung (`rungAgeBand`: from the end of the schooling the job takes, 18, 22 with a degree or 26 after medical school, or the job's own minimum age, three more years for each rung above the first, up to 70; a trainee's post, an intern, apprentice, junior or resident, only for twelve years after starting; a teen job's from its own minimum age, as it is held beside school) and near the player's age where the rung allows, so a 55-year-old pharmacy intern works beside young interns and a new graduate beside no 19-year-old therapist, named for the country and the era as the player's own rungs are (`rungTitle`), first names nobody else in the life has), linked to that job (`workplace`); event-made coworkers join the current job, hold a title from its ladder like hired ones (never "Unemployed") and are old enough for it (18+ beside a full-time job, 14+ beside a teen one); the title is the rung that best fits the age the card drew (a new hire of 22 is a trainee, not the CEO), and the age is pulled into that title's band (`titleAgeBand`) only when no rung fits. When the job ends (quit, fired, laid off, retired, jailed, switched), coworkers at 70+ relationship become friends ("Even after I left Ember Burger, Ana and I stayed friends.") and the rest leave the life. Coworkers from older saves have no workplace and stay.

## 11. Assets

24 definitions: 10 vehicles ($200-450k, depreciate 10-14%/yr, condition wears 3-8/yr), 8 homes ($120k-9M, appreciate ~3%/yr, mortgageable at 20% down over 25 years at 6%, rentable), 6 items. The shop sells what exists that year (`assetsForSale`): the electric car from 2012, the gaming PC from 1996 and a home computer before it. Buying adds happiness (2-22). Selling returns 95% of value minus any mortgage. Repairs restore condition. Upkeep is a fraction of price per year. Net worth = cash + assets − mortgages + investments − debts.

## 12. Activities

15 activities in three groups (mind & body, doctor, fun) with age gates, costs, energy, yearly caps, stat effects, karma and a risk roll (e.g. gym 4% injury, surgery 12% botch, vacation 6% food poisoning, party 10% "woke up somewhere unfamiliar"). The doctor costs $400 + per-illness fees (85% cheaper with public healthcare), heals +4 and cures each illness with its cure chance; rehab ($12k) cuts addictions by 40-70 and is refused, free of charge, without one. In prison only the gym (free in the yard), library, jogging, meditation and the doctor are open.

## 13. Health, illness, addiction

25 illnesses with age-band weights, severity 1-5, chronic flags, cure chances and treatment costs. Yearly new-illness chance 10% (child) / 13% (adult) / 28% (senior) × (1.3 − health/150), ×0.6 iron constitution, ×1.5 with a drug/alcohol addiction. Children with a living parent or guardian (step-parent, foster parent, or the aunt or uncle who took them in) get taken to the doctor automatically. Untreated illnesses drain health (chronic: severity−1; acute: 1.5×severity, capped at 6 total); treated chronic illnesses stop draining. Addictions (alcohol, drugs, gambling) are 0-100 levels set by events; ≥30 they creep up 0-6/yr, below they fade; ≥40 costs −3 happiness, −1 health and, for gambling, $500-4k a year. Only an addiction that took hold (reached 30, flag `habit:<kind>`) is "finally shook" when it fades; a party drink or two fades without a word. Appendicitis happens at most once a life (`oncePerLife`: never rolled again after it, or after an appendix card). Dementia, arthritis and osteoporosis are incurable (`incurable`): a doctor gives a plan to manage them and later checks on them ("It's stable for now."), but never cures them.

## 14. Crime and prison

Seven crimes in any year (shoplift, pickpocket, vandalism, burglary, car theft, online fraud – check fraud before 2000, bank robbery) with success chance (25-75%, +Smarts, +8% rebellious, +5% lucky, −notoriety), payouts, arrest chance on failure, sentences (0-20 years), karma hits and injury chances. Caught without a sentence → fine and a criminal record; caught with one → a court card (below); convicted → prison: job lost, school ended, no expenses but no income, 15%/yr yard fights, release logged. A new sentence while inside is added to the years left; arrest and court events written for the outside do not open in prison. From prison only calls and visits work (conversation, compliment, insult, break up); dates, gifts, money, proposals and babies wait for release, and no event brings a new partner. Records block many jobs and reduce interview odds.

**Court, juvenile court, parole** (`src/engine/systems/justice.ts`). An arrest that carries a sentence opens the court card `sys_trial` (a code-defined event in every registry, category crime), which names the charge and the possible sentence; the case lives in the card's context and the `trial` effect settles it:

| Choice | Acquittal chance | If convicted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plead guilty | none (karma +1) | sentence × 0.6, rounded, min 1 |
| Public defender (free) | 20% + max(0, Smarts − 50)/250 − notoriety/300 (3-60%) | full sentence |
| Hire a lawyer ($4k for a 1-year charge → $60k for 15+, country-scaled, paid up front; a minor's parents pay first, named as `{{parents}}` is: "Mom paid", "Aiden and I scraped together") | 60% → 45% as the charge grows, + (Smarts − 50)/500 − notoriety/400 (20-85%) | sentence × 0.8 (a 1- or 2-year charge rounds back to itself, so the verdict line claims no reduction) |
| Represent myself | 10% + (Smarts − 50)/150 (2-60%) | sentence × 1.3 |

The chips show the fee and the odds in words ("a long shot", "probably lose", "could go either way", "probably win", "almost sure to win"), never percentages. A lawyer the player cannot pay refuses, and the card comes back without that choice. An acquittal counts the arrest (notoriety +2) but leaves no record. Notoriety grows by 3 per year of the sentence passed (rounded, at least 1), never by the unrounded figure. Event `prison` effects narrate their own verdict and skip the court; after the event's lines the journal says when parole doubled that sentence or a minor serves it in juvenile detention (and for how long, if the cap cut it).

Under 18 the case goes to juvenile court, which asks for at most 2 years (the plea deal and a lawyer work down from that), and any sentence (from the court or an event) means **juvenile detention**: at most 2 years in all, out by 18, and school carries on inside ("I did my schoolwork in detention."; a diploma can be earned there). Only events with `{ inPrison: true }` and `{ juvenile: true }` open in detention, and those never open in adult prison. On release before 18 (or before the country's high school end age) without a diploma, a student goes back to school (a dropout does not). At 18 a juvenile record is sealed: `record` is cleared and the sealed convictions leave the obituary; an adult conviction stays.

**Parole.** Once an adult has served half of the current stay (sentences added inside lengthen it), each year has a 30% chance of a parole hearing card `sys_parole`, +5% per good-conduct write-up (`cp_prison_good_conduct`), −5% per infraction (`cp_prison_infraction`), +20% after a board recommendation (`cp_parole_recommended`), 10-80%. At the hearing, showing remorse or talking about plans is granted at 40% +7%/good conduct −8%/infraction +15% recommended (+10% remorse, or ± Smarts), or the player can waive it and serve the full time (no more hearings that stay). The content parole board (`cp_parole_hearing`) does not sit in a year the hearing does. Parole releases the player with the `parole` flag for the rest of that year and two full years after it; conduct write-ups are cleared at every release; a conviction while on parole counts double (the court card says so) and ends it. Every release writes a release-day line and sets the `released` flag for re-entry events.

No casino, lottery or drug menus in the MVP: any simulated-gambling feature makes the iOS app R18+ in Australia and 18+ everywhere if "frequent"; alcohol and drugs appear only as event temptations with consequences (see roadmap).

## 15. Random events

Events are data (`src/data/events/*.ts`) validated by `src/engine/events/schema.ts`:

```ts
{ id, category, title, text, minAge, maxAge, weight, once?, cooldown?, conditions?, person?, followUpOnly?,
  choices: [{ id, label, hint?, conditions?, effects: Effect[] }] }
```

Person selectors filter by kind, alive, relationship, age, `gender`, where someone lives (`farAway`), how recently they died (`diedWithin`) and whether they were known before a move abroad (`metBeforeMoveAbroad`), for the player's children `movedOut`, `married` and `kids`, and for pets `species`. Conditions cover stats, money, net worth, income, flags, job/category/level, school stage/grade, marital status, children, people of a kind, country, prison, juvenile detention, record, traits, gender, karma, fame, addictions, assets, hustles and hustle level, retirement, illness count and specific illnesses, years married, whether another event fired or how long ago, a specific job, lifetime counters, a real car, and money thresholds in USD scaled per country. Effects cover stat/money/karma/fame/energy deltas, journal lines, flags, relationship and love deltas, removing or killing the bound person, creating people (friend, lover, child, pet…, or a friend met far from home who lives there), death, prison, arrest, illness, cure, addiction, injury, job loss, performance, grade, expulsion, queued follow-ups, counters, weighted `random` outcomes, `chance` branches, asset damage/loss, achievements, marrying the bound partner (`marry`) and a child's milestones (`childMilestone`). Text supports `{{name}} {{person}} {{they}} {{parents}} {{grandma}} {{$1000}}`-style placeholders.

A new life opens with one newborn card (minAge 0: a first bath, the first night home) before the first age-up; heirs start without one. Selection per year: ages 0-2: 45% one event; 3-5: 75%; 6-17: 1 + 35% second; 18-69: 92% one, +40% second, +8% third; 70+: 1 + 30% second. Eligible = age range, conditions, `once`/`cooldown` (default 2 years), and a bindable person. Weighted pick without replacement, same-category repeats weighted ×0.25. Dead or departed bound people invalidate a queued event. While the player is in prison only events written for prison (`inPrison: true`) open. Money in events (effects and `money`/`netWorth`/`income` thresholds) is authored in USD and scaled per country; a minor's costs fall on their parents unless a choice spends the child's own savings (`payer: 'self'`), and the parents also pay for a child's doctor visits and outings.

Content budget: 12 category files (early childhood, kid years, teen years, young adult, adult life, later life, work, love & family, health, money, crime & prison, friends & strangers), ≥ 300 choice events at launch, plus 50 flavour lines so no year is empty. House style: first person, 15-45 words, choices ≤ 6 words, no dominant choice, Teen rating.

## 15a. Journal flavour lines

One-line, no-choice journal lines (`src/data/flavor.ts`, 141 lines: 52 generic, 89 situational) keep the journal personal between event cards. A generic line has only an age range. A situational line adds `conditions` (the event condition language, including `hasIllness`, `yearsMarried` and `hustleLevel`) and may bind a `person` for `{{person}}`/`{{they}}`. Situational lines cover prison years, being locked up as a minor, university, graduate school, retirement, 10/25/40-year anniversaries and long marriages, pets, thirteen chronic illnesses, an addiction past 30 (where it starts to grow), a side hustle still stuck at level 0 (earning nothing), unemployment and debt (net worth below zero), single parents of young children, grandparents and 90+.

The engine (`src/engine/systems/flavor.ts`) picks the line after the year's systems have run and its cards are scheduled:

- **Quiet year** (no other journal line): always one line, from the generic lines in the age range plus every situational line whose situation holds, situational lines weighted 3x.
- **Busy year**: 35% chance of one situational line (never a generic one), unless the year brought grave news (a death, a new severity-4+ illness, the doctor's warning).
- A situational line is only written when its conditions hold both at the start of the year (just after the birthday) and after its systems ran, and a bound person was already in that role at the start: no line about a diagnosis, release, divorce or pet the year's own lines just reported. In prison only lines written for prison (`{ inPrison: true }`) qualify, as with events, and the year of a release (it began behind bars) gets no line about life outside.
- Lines that say the year was uneventful are marked `quiet` and are only written in a year with no other line and no event card. The line is chosen at the birthday, so if the player's own doings fill the year after all (a crime and its court card, a visit, a baby), it is taken back at the next birthday, or when a death at that birthday or during the year ends the life (`settleQuietYear`); a quiet line that is still its year's only line stays. Only the year just started shifts in the log, so the journal's existing year cards keep their keys.
- No line repeats within 10 years (`GameState.flavorSeen`, optional, pruned to the window); a quiet year whose candidates were all used recently takes the one written longest ago, so no year is empty.

The validator checks every flavour line on each run (wording, placeholders, first person, ≤ 100 characters, quiet wording without the `quiet` mark, unknown illness ids) and that each one fits some sampled year; the text audit renders them all in `audit/text/flavor.md`. The samplers also take a few choices the policies never make on some seeds (graduate school, a side hustle, tending a marriage, a habit past 30; `scripts/varied-lives.ts`) so that content gated on them is reachable.

## 16. Death, obituary, ribbons, legacy

On death: cause (illness-driven where one exists; otherwise age-appropriate), net worth, one **ribbon** from 26 by priority (Gone Too Soon for a child, Deadly, Jailbird, Tycoon, Famous, Rich, Academic, Family First, Heartbreaker, Cunning, Scandalous, Fit, Bookworm, Globetrotter, Generous, Loyal, Hustler, Centenarian, Survivor (15+ illnesses), Addict, Lazy, Wasted, Successful, Happy, Miserable, Mediocre; money thresholds scale with the country's salary multiplier), an **obituary** generated from career, education, family, money and convictions, and an epitaph. The career it tells is the one retired from or the one of the most years (all its employers and rungs together), never just the best paid; a long career before a short last job is named beside it ("He spent 24 years as a staff engineer and later worked as a help desk technician at Clearwater Ltd."). The tombstone screen shows all of it plus karma. The graveyard keeps the last 200 lives and the ribbon collection; **Clear graveyard** on that screen deletes them all after a confirmation (the privacy policy promises this).

**Legacy:** continue as any living child: the new life starts as that child (their name, gender, age, looks/smarts/health, country and city) in the year of the death, with their share of the estate (80% of the net worth split between the children; a minor heir's goes into their savings, as the journal says), the family tree (the dead player becomes a dead parent; the child's recorded other parent, mother or father by their own gender, and siblings persist; no parent is invented; the dead player's living parents become grandparents, and a grown heir keeps the spouse and children §10a gave them), generation counter and ancestor list. Everyone carried over stays who they were (name, gender, age, job, looks, smarts, health, temper, money, and whether and how they died) and only how they are related changes: the dead parent keeps the job they died in (else their last one), the stats they died with and the bond they had with this child. Parents, grandparents and older siblings are known since birth; a younger sibling was born when the heir was that much older ("Born when you were 11."); a spouse is known at least since their eldest child was born. The heir card shows a child still at school as "Student" and one who left high school without a diploma as "No diploma" (a child out of school has no job to show); brothers and sisters keep those milestones (`growth`) in the new life, so their People rows agree with the cards. A grown heir starts between jobs with this year's job listings open (from 14), and every adult year before the takeover (from 18 or the end of high school) counts as a year worked and as general experience, so the obituary and the Lazy ribbon judge only the years played; no job or employer is carried over, because the previous life never gives the player's children one. Free play: one hand-off per family line (generation 1 → 2); premium: unlimited. **Second chance:** once per life, death can be undone (rewarded ad, or instantly for premium): health is restored to ~45 and severe illnesses removed.

## 16a. Achievements

Forty achievements (`src/data/achievements.ts`) are meta progression across lives: life milestones (a degree, graduate school, practicing medicine, the top rung of a full-time career, marriage, a 50-year marriage, three children, a home, a mansion, a local-terms million, a first pet (one of my own: not a family pet that was there when I was born), a close friend met 40 years ago, retirement, 100 and 110 years), ways to live (die at 80+ without ever committing a crime or being arrested, though the arrest a false theft charge brings (`cp_false_charge_court`: the plea, or a lost trial) does not count against it, three prison sentences, paying off $20k+ of debt including student loans without bankruptcy, 80 fame, a top-level hustle, 95 karma, a scholarship, 10 vacations, 100 workouts, 50 compliments, a millionaire without a diploma), the family line (generation 2 and 5), across lives (finish 1 and 10 lives, 10 different ribbons, lives in 5 countries) and seven secrets shown as "???" until found (five pets in one life, fired three times, a rejected proposal, three divorces, a pigeon, bankruptcy, three cosmetic surgeries). Money thresholds scale with the country's salary multiplier, like the ribbons. Millionaire's target is set in the local currency the player sees: a million US dollars at local wages, rounded to two significant figures and never less than a million of the local currency ($1,000,000, £1,000,000, ₹12,000,000, ₦150,000,000), and Debt Free's is $20,000 at local wages rounded the same way (£13,000, ₹250,000); achievements that name an amount show it in the current life's currency and test the amount as shown. Full Ride needs a scholarship actually won, not a wealthy family paying.

Each achievement is a pure test over the current life plus a summary of the device's other lives (distinct ribbons and countries, lives finished, from the graveyard). The store runs the checker (`src/engine/systems/achievements.ts`) after every age-up, action, event choice, rewarded reward, new life and heir hand-off, death included; already unlocked ones are skipped, so a check is a few microseconds. An event can also grant one with the `{ achievement: id }` effect (the validator rejects unknown ids). Three facts the checker needs are kept in `flags` so old saves need no migration: `peakDebt` (largest loans plus student debt, recorded by the finance pass and before a debt payment), `proposalsRejected` (the propose interaction) and `cosmeticSurgeries` (the plastic surgery activity, botched or not; the lifetime `surgeries` counter also counts operations from health events). A "dropout" is an adult without a diploma who is no longer in school, so a student whose high school runs to 19 does not count at 18.

Unlocks persist in the settings store under their own storage key as `{ unlockedAt, lifeName, age }` per id. They survive new lives, premium changes and Clear graveyard; Settings → Reset achievements (with a confirmation) locks them all again. A new unlock shows as a trophy toast in the app-wide feedback host ("Achievement unlocked: …", or "3 achievements unlocked" for a batch); it waits while an event card is open, goes before the next year recap instead of being dropped by it, and outlives the end of a life, and it is not written to the journal. The achievements screen (home screen and tombstone) lists them by group with a progress count; the tombstone button also says how many this life unlocked. Dynasty (generation 5) needs more than the one free hand-off per family line.

## 19. Monetization

**Ads (free users only), `src/services/ads/policy.ts`:**

- Anchored adaptive banner on the life screen only (hidden for premium).
- Interstitials: never during the first 12 age-ups on the device, never within 90 s of launch, then at most one per 6 age-ups, ≥ 150 s apart, ≤ 3 per session, only on the Age+ tap (never over a choice card, never on launch, never on death).
- Rewarded (opt-in, capped per day): Windfall cash (3/day; max of $800, 25% of salary, 5% of cash), Stat boost (+30 Happiness or Health, or +16 Smarts or Looks, up to the stat's cap or natural limit; each button shows the real gain, and a stat already at its limit is not offered and never uses up a boost; 3/day), Second chance after death (1/day, once per life), New job listings (3/day). Premium users get the same rewards instantly without an ad. Rewarded buttons appear only once the ads SDK has initialised, and a tap that does not pay out (no ad loaded, ad closed early, daily cap reached) says why under the button.
- Consent: UMP form in regulated regions, ATT on iOS, Teen max ad content rating, no child-directed tagging, privacy options in Settings.

**Premium (RevenueCat entitlement `premium`, product `lifeloop_premium`, one-time, $4.99 USD):** no ads, custom starting stats and wealthy start, unlimited generations, instant second chances, all future premium perks. ($4.99 matches Another Life's verified ad-free price and BitLife's launch price; BitLife's current $7.99-9.99 reflects eight years of content we do not yet have.) Restore purchases in Settings and on the paywall (where purchases are not available, such as the web build, both say so instead of offering it).

**What stays free forever:** every event, career, country, relationship, asset, activity, crime, ribbon, one generation hand-off per family line, and all future core content. No consumables, no loot boxes, no subscriptions at launch.

## 20a. Design-panel decisions

Three independent designs (parity-first, retention-first, systems-first) were scored by two judges; both picked the systems-first design, which is the one built on this engine, and asked for a few grafts. See `DESIGN_PANEL_SYNTHESIS.md` for the full proposal. Adopted now:

- **Year summary toast** after each age-up: stat deltas and cash change, shown after the year's event cards close (never a mandatory modal).
- **Yearly energy** raised to 1 / 2 / 4 / 3 pips (ages 0-5 / 6-12 / 13-59 / 60+) so a year allows 3-4 social or physical actions; money and admin actions stay free and Age+ is never blocked.
- One premium SKU at $4.99 with the existing ids; one free generation hand-off per family line.

1. **Content cadence:** bi-weekly data drops (20-40 events) via EAS Update; 600+ events within 90 days; weekly challenges and daily goals built on the same condition language.
2. **Perk draws** (Mind Vacation / Life Restart pattern) on top of the starting scenarios of §4a, and scenario content: foster-care, immigrant and farm events (see §4a).
3. **Casino and mature pack** as a separately rated decision (would move the rating to 18+ in several regions).
4. **Fame track** (social media, endorsements), **business ownership**, **real-estate landlord** depth, pets expansion, emigration, extended family beyond grandparents and grandchildren (cousins, nieces and nephews).
5. **Life Points meta-currency** across lives unlocking cosmetic/perk items (Another Life) — only if it does not become a paywall.
6. Share cards (tombstone and "life receipt"), localisation, accessibility pass (VoiceOver labels on bars and buttons).

## 22. Risks

- **Content thinness** is the top failure mode of clones: the validator, registry generator and property tests make content drops cheap; keep the event pool growing.
- **Balance drift:** every tuning change must keep `npm test` green (death ages, net-worth ordering, no empty years).
- **Ad fatigue:** the policy numbers live in `src/config/monetization.ts`; never raise them silently.
- **Rating creep:** adding casino or drug menus changes the age rating; re-run questionnaires.
- **Trademark:** never use competitor names in metadata; choose a final name after a trademark search.
